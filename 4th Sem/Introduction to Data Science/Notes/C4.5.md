It uses **Gain Ratio** as its test attribute. It is an improved version of Information Gain 
It helps mitigates bias that information gain has towards attributes with many distinct values. 

*Bias in information gain:*

- Information Gain measures how much uncertainty is reduced when splitting the data based on a particular attribute 
- However, it tends to favor attributes with a large number of distinct values, even if they are not meaningful for classification 

*Gain Ratio Normalization*:

- It adjusts the information gain by dividing it by the split information, which represents how broadly the data is split 
$$ SplitInfo_A(D) = - \sum_{j = 1}^{v} {\frac{|Dj|}{|D|} \times log_2(\frac{|Dj|}{|D|}}) $$
$$GainRatio(A) = \frac{Gain(A)}{SplitInfo(A)}$$

*Effects of Gain Ratio*

- By penalizing splits that create many small partitions, gain ratio avoids favoring attributes with numerous unique values. 
- It ensures the selected attributes provide meaningful splits rather than just dividing the data into very small subsets 


This **Gain Ratio** is used in C4.5, which is a successor of ID3.
